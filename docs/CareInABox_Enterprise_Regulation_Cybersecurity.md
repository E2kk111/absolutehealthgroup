# Care-in-a-Box™ — Enterprise Operations Offers

## Commercial positioning

Keep the existing clinical Care-in-a-Box™ application portfolio and add a **separate Enterprise Operations grouping**:

1. **Regulation-in-a-Box™ — powered by RegOS™**
   - Promise: *Know what changed. Know what applies. Know what to do. Prove what was done.*
   - Buyer: Compliance officers, healthcare operations leaders, regulatory affairs and clinical governance.
   - Problem: Unclear obligations, fragmented policy change tracking, missing audit evidence and manual corrective-action tracking.
   - Product: Regulatory readiness assessment, requirement/obligation mapping, versioned rule review, evidence tracking and human-approved remediation.
   - Deliverables: Obligation register, regulatory change log, owner/approval map, remediation tracker, audit-ready evidence package.
   - Pilot: One physician group or post-acute operator, one applicable regulation set, baseline gaps, assigned owners and documented before/after readiness.
   - Boundary: RegOS™ assists with rule interpretation and approved workflow execution; responsible compliance professionals approve consequential interpretations and actions.

2. **Cybersecurity-in-a-Box™ — proposed LokDon technology evaluation**
   - Promise: *Protect sensitive data. Govern access. Detect risk. Demonstrate security controls.*
   - Buyer: CIO, CISO, IT, privacy, risk and healthcare security leadership.
   - Problem: Fragmented security evidence, unverified safeguards, access risks and difficult remediation tracking.
   - Product: Security risk assessment, encryption/identity technology evaluation, control inventory, incident-response workflow and evidence tracking.
   - Deliverables: Risk assessment, security control gap register, integration test plan, remediation record and documented safeguards.
   - Pilot: Controlled evaluation with non-production data, a baseline security assessment and independently verified test results before any PHI integration.
   - Boundary: No asserted LokDon partnership, executed integration, HIPAA certification, regulatory approval or validated cryptographic/post-quantum claims without documentation.

## Canonical architecture — unchanged

Customer problem → Care-in-a-Box™ commercial solution → Medical Navigator™ (where clinical navigation applies) → AION™ Intelligence + Authority → human authority boundary → RegOS™ approved controls → GitHealth™ governed execution → ShieldAI™/Prove™ evidence → ZScore™ and Cost-to-Goal™/Economics™ as applicable.

**Commercial surface:** Problem → Product → Outcome → Proof → Economics.

**Implementation principle:** Reuse the existing infrastructure. Do not create a new top-level architecture layer or imply that security controls automatically constitute clinical authorization.

## Pilot gates

- Named customer sponsor and workflow owner
- Defined scope, baseline, risk/obligation register and success criteria
- Signed-off access controls, data handling, and vendor security review
- Human sign-off for consequential regulatory/security decisions
- Evidence packet and remediation history with timestamps and responsible actors
- Outcome and economics measured against baseline; do not use hypothetical values as verified ROI

## Website claims policy

Use **"proposed", "subject to evaluation", "designed to support"** for unimplemented cybersecurity features. Do not say **"HIPAA-certified", "LokDon partner", "post-quantum proven", "automatically compliant", "FDA/CMS approved"** without substantiation.

**Portfolio count note:** The existing website lists nine institutional applications (Specialty, PIN, ACCESS, CJR-X, Burn, Wound, Rural, Senior and Regenerative/Longevity). The eight categories in the marketing visual are broader clinical groupings. Preserve both taxonomies rather than inaccurately calling the website's nine applications eight or claiming exactly ten total applications.
